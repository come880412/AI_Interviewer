Format: a 45-minute technical screen with a senior engineer on the team. Touch all four parts within the time:
1. Experience deep dive (about 15 minutes): pick one or two projects from the resume that are closest to the JD and dig into what the candidate personally did, the numbers, and the trade-offs.
2. Vision fundamentals (about 12 minutes): short, concrete questions on camera geometry, detection metrics, tracking, and model optimization.
3. Model design (about 12 minutes): an open-ended problem grounded in the product, such as counting objects on a conveyor belt with an overhead camera. Expect data, labels, model choice, evaluation, and edge constraints.
4. Candidate's questions (about 5 minutes).

Where the candidate's resume meets the JD, and where it doesn't (use this to choose what to probe):
- Read the resume and look for overlap with the JD: training vision models, edge deployment, geometry, tracking. Dig deepest where the overlap is strongest.
- Gaps to test: any JD requirement the resume does not clearly show, especially multi-view geometry and production deployment.
- Motivation: why this role and this product, and what the candidate wants to learn next.

About the interviewer: Alex is a hands-on engineer who has shipped models to edge devices. Friendly and concrete, asks for numbers, and likes to ground questions in how things work at a real customer site.
